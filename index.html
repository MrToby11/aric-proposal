01 — The Gap

Industry runs on hardware.
Classrooms teach theory.

Business and industry are being reshaped by AI and robotics, and the demand for graduates with genuine hardware skills has risen sharply alongside them. In higher education, those skills are rarely the focus.

Classroom learning remains largely theoretical. Students study distributed systems without configuring one, model machine learning workloads they cannot run at scale, and graduate having never integrated a robot into a working process. The equipment that would close that gap is simply too expensive for a single department to justify.

Without substantial investment in this kind of infrastructure, UFV graduates face a widening competency gap the moment they enter enterprise technical environments.

US$200,000

The cost of a single eight-GPU server built on NVIDIA H200 NVL cards, which run over US$25,000 each and draw 5–7 kW under load.

Few universities can put equipment at that price point in front of an undergraduate. ARIC is designed so that UFV can.

02 — The Proposal

One facility, three commitments

A multidisciplinary computing and automated-systems hub, responding to the convergence of AI, high-performance computing and physical robotics across every faculty at UFV.

01

Physical infrastructure

A multi-storey, LEED Gold certified building on a dedicated site fifteen minutes from the Abbotsford campus — purpose-built with high-density server rooms, AI software labs, and interactive robotics assembly and testing arenas.

02

Enterprise hardware

Advanced GPU clusters, local high-throughput storage networks, and industrial and educational robotics — including automated logistics hardware, AMO robotics and service robots configured for real enterprise applications.

03

Applied curriculum

A co-developed undergraduate specialization track and applied certification courses covering AI system deployment, hardware configuration, and human–robot integration in business environments.

03 — The Return

What $100M buys beyond a building

Applied research revenue

Research collaborations with industrial, agricultural and logistics partners across the Fraser Valley, developing custom automation solutions through sponsored applied research.

Enrolment growth

Expanded domestic and international enrolment across CIS and STEM programs, drawn by access to laboratory and research equipment few comparable institutions can offer.

Graduate market value

UFV positioned as a pipeline for workforce-ready systems administrators, AI specialists, computing professionals and robotics integration engineers.

04 — The Site

151.31 acres on Huntingdon Road

Two parcels at 28310 Huntingdon Road, Abbotsford — more than double the footprint of the current UFV Abbotsford campus, and roughly a fifteen-minute drive from it.

The scale matters. High-density server halls, robotics testing arenas and the power and cooling plant they depend on are difficult to retrofit into an existing campus. A dedicated site allows the facility to be built correctly the first time, with room for the next decade of expansion.

Acquisition cost: $16.85M of the $100M envelope.

05 — The Timeline

Four years, with the brakes built in

Four kill points stand between approval and the September 2030 handover. If the project cannot clear one, it is redesigned, delayed or stopped — before the next tranche of public money is committed.

  1. Kill Point 1

    Feasibility

    Can UFV realistically build and operate the centre within the time and the $100M available? If not, redesign or terminate.

  2. Kill Point 2

    Public consultation

    Surveys, public meetings and stakeholder sessions test community support before the design is finalised. Unresolvable opposition stops the project.

  3. Kill Point 3

    Design & budget approval

    Building layout, laboratory requirements, HPC infrastructure and updated cost estimates reviewed against the approved envelope.

  4. Kill Point 4

    Technology installation

    Networking, robotics and laboratory systems verified against technical requirements before full commissioning.

06 — Stewardship

$85M planned. $15M held back.

A $100M grant is public money, and the plan treats it that way. Eighty-five million is allocated across land, construction, computing infrastructure, laboratories and staffing. The remaining fifteen million stays in a contingency reserve — tied to modular system upgrades on a 36-month cycle, because hardware at this price point depreciates fast.

Spending is tracked against earned value, so cost and schedule variance surface early rather than at handover. Every material change to scope, schedule or budget goes through formal change control and back to UFV for approval before any money moves.
