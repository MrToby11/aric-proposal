02 — Cost Justification

Where the larger figures come from

Five categories account for $68.85M of the $85M planned. Each is anchored to a published source rather than an assumption.

$22M

Building construction & site development

University teaching buildings in Vancouver are estimated at roughly $750–$1,250 per square foot. No equivalent figure is published for Abbotsford, so this provides a basis for preliminary planning only — the final cost will depend on the facility's size, design and site requirements.

Source: Altus Group, 2026 Canadian Construction Costing Guide (reference 4).

$17M

HPC / GPU / computing infrastructure

NVIDIA H200 NVL GPUs cost over US$25,000 each, and an eight-GPU server can exceed US$200,000. These figures show the scale of investment required to establish genuine AI computing capability, and support the allocation proposed here.

Source: Exxact Corporation (2025) (reference 6).

$16.85M

Land acquisition

Two parcels at 28310 Huntingdon Road, Abbotsford, totalling approximately 151.31 acres at an asking price of $16.85M CAD. That is more than double the size of the current UFV Abbotsford campus, roughly a fifteen-minute drive away, and more than enough space for the proposed facility and its future expansion.

Source: Fraser Valley MLS listing.

$10M

Research laboratories & specialized equipment

The University of Toronto's guide to construction estimates distinguishes building construction from equipment and other project expenses. That distinction is why laboratory equipment is budgeted separately here rather than folded into the construction line.

Source: University of Toronto, A Practical Guide to Addressing Construction & Renovation (2024) (reference 5).

$3M

Sustainability, power & cooling

A single eight-GPU H200 server draws approximately 5–7 kW, creating substantial electrical and heat-management requirements. This allocation covers the electrical capacity and the efficient cooling systems that load demands — including direct-to-chip liquid cooling, which saves up to 40% of cooling energy against conventional air-cooled rooms.

Source: Exxact Corporation (2025) (reference 6).

$15M

Contingency reserve

Held back rather than allocated, and tied directly to the cost of modular systems. It is drawn on for upgrades at a frequency of roughly 36 months, which is the practical response to rapid hardware obsolescence on a project that will not open its doors until 2030.

See risk R-03, rapid hardware obsolescence.

03 — Cost Control

Tracking spend against earned value

Six earned-value metrics, chosen so that cost and schedule problems surface while there is still time to act on them rather than at handover.

PV

Planned Value

How much work, and how much budget, was planned to be completed by this point.

AC

Actual Cost

Total costs incurred, direct and indirect.

EV

Earned Value

The physical results actually completed, valued at budget.

CV

Cost Variance

CV = EV − AC

If CV is negative, the project is over budget.

SV

Schedule Variance

SV = EV − PV

If SV is negative, the project is behind schedule.

CPI

Cost Performance Index

CPI = EV ÷ AC

If CPI is below 1, spending is less efficient than planned.

A note on what these figures are. Given our limited experience and our lack of access to real project data, we cannot produce accurate calculations or results at this stage. These metrics are put forward as the cost control methods we would apply, not as performance measurements we have taken.

04 — Change Control

Nothing changes without going through this

Over four years there will be requests to change the plan — new research equipment, an expanded laboratory, an altered building design. Each one is reviewed before approval, in the same spirit as the kill points.

  1. Request raised A change to scope, schedule, budget or design is formally proposed.
  2. Impact assessed The Project Manager evaluates the effect on scope, timeline, budget and overall quality.
  3. Decision by UFV Findings are presented to UFV, which approves or rejects the request.
  4. Documents updated If approved, the budget, schedule and all related documents are revised first.
  5. Change implemented Only then does the work proceed against the revised baseline.

With $100M committed and a fixed deadline of September 1, 2030, this process matters. It is the mechanism that prevents unnecessary spending, schedule slippage and scope creep — and it is what keeps the out-of-scope boundary on the plan page meaningful rather than aspirational.
