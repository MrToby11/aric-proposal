Project Management Pre-Plan

What gets built, in what order, and when we stop

The scope boundary, the full work breakdown, the schedule through to September 2030, and the four kill points at which this project can be redesigned, delayed or terminated.

Scope Work breakdown Schedule Kill points

01 — Scope

The boundary

What the $100M covers, and—just as importantly—what it does not.

Project objective

To design, build, equip and launch a research and innovation centre at UFV by September 1, 2030 using the provided $100 million in funding, providing shared research infrastructure for students, faculty, researchers and industry partners.

In scope

Research & technology infrastructure

  • High-performance computing (HPC) and GPU infrastructure
  • AI and machine learning research facilities
  • Robotics and automation laboratories
  • Research data infrastructure
  • High-speed networking and cybersecurity infrastructure

Additional faculty research labs

  • Agriculture technology
  • Environmental and climate research
  • Health technology
  • Computational biology
  • Business analytics
  • AR/VR and digital technology

Innovation & prototyping

  • Makerspace
  • 3D printing
  • Electronics prototyping
  • Robotics development areas
  • Collaboration spaces

Student research

  • Student research projects
  • Research assistant opportunities
  • Access to computing resources

UFV already identifies applied research as an area that gives students opportunities for experiential learning and relationships with industry and community stakeholders. ARIC extends that commitment rather than starting a new one.

Building & operations

  • New research centre building
  • Research offices
  • Meeting rooms
  • Training and classroom spaces
  • Server and computing rooms
  • Sustainable energy and cooling systems
  • Accessibility infrastructure

Out of scope

  • Redevelopment of the entire UFV campus
  • Replacement of existing UFV facilities
  • Construction of unrelated academic buildings
  • Large-scale residential facilities
  • Permanent funding of all future research projects

These exclusions exist to prevent scope creep. Any request to cross this boundary goes through formal change control before it is costed or scheduled.

03 — Schedule

2026 Q4 to 2030 Q3

Eight phases and six scheduling milestones, from project charter through to final close-out, with a schedule buffer held before the September 1, 2030 deadline.

On the schedule buffer

A buffer is held at the end of the schedule to absorb uncertainty: construction and permit delays, equipment shipping and installation, vendor issues, and testing failures are all plausible on a project of this length.

We should be straightforward about the risk here. The chart allows a little over a year for construction. Judging by how long UFV's recent dormitory and cafeteria builds took, the building alone could realistically take closer to two years. The schedule as drawn is achievable but optimistic, and construction is the line item most likely to need more time than it has been given.

Sept 1, 2030

The fixed project deadline. Every phase above is scheduled backwards from it, and the buffer sits immediately before it rather than being distributed across earlier phases — so that slippage is visible against a single, unmoving date.

04 — Kill Points

Four places this project can be stopped

A kill point is a scheduled decision to continue, change or terminate. Each one sits before a major commitment of funds, so that a project that is no longer viable is stopped while the money is still in the bank.

  1. Kill Point 1

    Feasibility

    Can UFV realistically build and operate the centre within the given time and the $100M available? This gate tests the business case against the constraints before any design work is commissioned.

    If it clears
    Proceed to requirements and design.
    If it does not
    Redesign the project, or terminate it.
  2. Kill Point 2

    Public consultation

    Before the final design proceeds, UFV will run public consultations to understand concerns from students, faculty, local residents and community organisations. This project could be genuinely controversial: opposition may come from concerns about AI replacing jobs, data privacy, high energy consumption, and the environmental impact of advanced computing facilities.

    Feedback will be collected through surveys, public meetings and stakeholder discussions to gauge the real level of community support. Where significant opposition is identified, the Project Manager will review possible responses — responsible AI policies, improved energy efficiency, and greater transparency about the centre's research activities.

    If it clears
    Finalise the design and proceed to approval.
    If it does not
    Redesign, delay or cancel — before major construction investment is made.
  3. Kill Point 3

    Design & budget approval

    Once the architectural and technical designs are complete, UFV reviews the building layout, research laboratory requirements, HPC infrastructure, construction plans and updated cost estimates against the approved envelope.

    If it clears
    Proceed to procurement and construction.
    If it does not
    Modify the design, reduce scope, or reconsider the project before proceeding.
  4. Kill Point 4

    Technology installation review

    The project team reviews the installed technology — networking infrastructure, robotics equipment and research laboratories — against the technical requirements, before the facility moves into full system testing and commissioning.

    If it clears
    Proceed to full system testing and commissioning.
    If it does not
    Resolve the installation problems before any further progress.
