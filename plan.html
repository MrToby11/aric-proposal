Project Management Pre-Plan

What gets built, in what order, and when we stop

The scope boundary, the full work breakdown, the schedule through to September 2030, and the four kill points at which this project can be redesigned, delayed or terminated.

Scope Work breakdown Schedule Kill points

01 — Scope

The boundary

What the $100M covers, and what it does not.

Project objective

To design, build, equip, and launch a research and innovation centre at UFV by September 1, 2030, using the provided $100 million funding. The centre will provide shared research infrastructure for students, faculty, researchers, and industry partners.

In scope

Inspiration for this part is taken from aiicenter.com[3]

Research and Technology Infrastructure

  • High-performance computing (HPC) and GPU infrastructure
  • AI and machine learning research facilities
  • Robotics and automation laboratories
  • Research data infrastructure
  • High-speed networking and cybersecurity infrastructure

Additional Faculty Research Labs

  • Agriculture technology
  • Environmental and climate research
  • Health technology
  • Computational biology
  • Business analytics
  • AR/VR and digital technology

Innovation and Prototyping

  • Makerspace
  • 3D printing
  • Electronics prototyping
  • Robotics development areas
  • Collaboration spaces

Student Research

  • Student research projects
  • Research assistant opportunities
  • Access to computing resources

UFV already identifies applied research as an area that provides students with opportunities to participate in research, experiential learning and relationships with industry and community stakeholders.

Building and Operations

  • New research centre building
  • Research offices
  • Meeting rooms
  • Training/classroom spaces
  • Server/computing rooms
  • Sustainable energy and cooling systems
  • Accessibility infrastructure

Out of scope

  • Redevelopment of the entire UFV campus
  • Replacement of existing UFV facilities
  • Construction of unrelated academic buildings
  • Large scale residential facilities
  • Permanent funding of all future research projects

04 — Kill Points

Four places this project can be stopped

A kill point is a scheduled decision to continue, change or terminate. Each one sits before a major commitment of funds, so that a project that is no longer viable is stopped while the money is still in the bank.

  1. Kill Point 1

    Feasibility

    Can UFV realistically build and operate the centre within the given time and $100M funding? If YES then continue to requirements/design, otherwise terminate or redesign the project.

  2. Kill Point 2

    The project could be controversial in the eyes of the public

    Before proceeding with the final design, UFV will conduct public consultations to understand concerns from students, faculty, local residents, and community organizations regarding the development of the centre. Some stakeholders may oppose the project due to concerns about AI replacing jobs, data privacy, high energy consumption, and the environmental impact of advanced computing facilities.

    The project team will collect feedback through surveys, public meetings, and stakeholder discussions to evaluate the level of community support. If significant opposition is identified, the Project Manager will review possible solutions, such as implementing responsible AI policies, improving energy efficiency, and providing greater transparency about the centre's research activities. If these concerns cannot be addressed, the project may need to be redesigned, delayed, or cancelled before major construction investments are made.

  3. Kill Point 3

    Design and budget approval

    Once the architectural and technical designs are completed. UFV will review the building layout, research laboratory requirements, HPC infrastructure, construction plans, and updated cost estimates. If the design doesn't meets the project's objectives, exceed the approved budget or major issues are identified, the team may need to modify the design, reduce the scope, or reconsider the project before proceeding.

  4. Kill Point 4

    Technology installation review

    The project team will review the tech systems including networking infrastructure, robotics equipment, and research laboratories to ensure they meet the technical requirements. If major installation problems are found, the team will need to resolve them before proceeding with full system testing and commissioning.
