Stakeholders, Governance & Risk

Who this affects, and what could go wrong

A $100M facility changes things for people who never asked for it. This page sets out who has a stake, who is accountable, the five risks we consider material, and the commitments that constrain how the centre operates.

Stakeholders Organization SWOT Risk register Ethics & sustainability

01 — Stakeholders

Internal and external

Seven stakeholder groups, each with a different definition of what success looks like.

Internal

Primary users

Students

Need uninterrupted access to the labs, high-speed computing and current robotics hardware to complete coursework and capstone projects. If the facility is hard to book or its equipment is out of date, it has failed its main purpose.

Curriculum owners

Faculty & academic leadership

Accountable for curriculum design and for building applied research opportunities into degree courses, so that the centre is embedded in what students study rather than sitting alongside it.

Operations

Campus facilities & maintenance

Responsible for engaging builders and for maintaining, repairing and replacing campus infrastructure as required — including a new site with demands unlike anything currently on campus.

Operations

Campus security & support

Handling incidents that affect campus operations, across a facility holding substantial high-value computing hardware on a separate site.

External

Funding partners

Federal & provincial government

Emphasis on regional economic development, digital readiness of the workforce, and measurable educational outcomes. They are the reason the money exists, and the audience this proposal is addressed to.

Industry

Co-op placement sponsors

Regional employers in logistics, manufacturing and hospitality who will host co-op placements, sponsor applied research projects, and hire graduates.

Neighbours

Local Fraser Valley community

Neighbouring communities who are owed clear communication about environmental impact and zoning. Their support is not assumed — it is tested at Kill Point 2.

02 — Project Organization

Five departments under one project manager

The Project Manager owns the three constraints — scope, time and cost — and reports to the executive champion. Each department below owns a distinct slice of delivery.

Financial

Budget, cost tracking and financial reporting. Also responsible for finding partnerships and potential sponsorship, working through to industry partners.

Design & construction

Architectural design, engineering and construction, with procurement reporting into it.

Technology

Purchasing and standing up HPC, GPUs, networking, cybersecurity and IT infrastructure, covering IT, HPC and robotics.

Research

Represents the research requirements of the different faculties, and carries student needs and usability through faculty and student representatives.

Governance, ethics & safety

Enforces the guardrails: academic integrity, data privacy, transparency, fairness, accountability and security standards.

03 — SWOT

Strategic position

Strengths and weaknesses are internal to UFV; opportunities and threats come from outside it.

Internal · Helpful

Strengths

  • Established academic breadth, with solid foundational CIS courses across several departments
  • Existing interdisciplinary collaboration between faculties
  • Major new capital investment in a purpose-built facility

Internal · Harmful

Weaknesses

  • Significant level of power consumption
  • High initial demands on vendors
  • High initial capital costs for advanced computing and research infrastructure

External · Helpful

Opportunities

  • Applied robotics and AI leadership at the national level
  • Funding from industry and corporate donations
  • Cross-disciplinary collaboration within faculties
  • Increased student research and experiential learning

External · Harmful

Threats

  • High rate of technology and hardware obsolescence
  • Community resistance to automation and job loss
  • Potential construction and project cost overruns

R-01

Public environmental scrutiny

  • External / Public relations
  • High severity
  • Medium probability

Mitigation: Address the computing footprint directly rather than defensively — binding environmental commitments, on-site solar supplementation, and liquid cooling infrastructure that measurably reduces operational energy draw.

R-02

Hardware supply chain delays

  • Operations / Procurement
  • High severity
  • High probability

Mitigation: Establish multi-vendor procurement paths during Phase 1 rather than committing to a single supplier, and secure advance priority allocation for specialized GPU architectures — the components with the longest and least predictable lead times.

R-03

Rapid hardware obsolescence

  • Technical / Financial
  • Medium severity
  • High probability

Mitigation: A dedicated $15M reserve fund, tied directly to the cost of the modular systems and reserved solely for upgrades on a 36-month cycle. The facility does not open until 2030; hardware specified today will not be current then, and the budget is built on that assumption.

R-04

Scope creep across departments

  • Management
  • Medium severity
  • Medium probability

Mitigation: Formal change control procedures, with the project management team approving and tracking any new hardware request. The out-of-scope list exists for this reason.

R-05

Community pushback on AI job displacement

  • External / Public relations
  • Medium severity
  • High probability

Mitigation: Public outreach workshops that engage local communities on their actual concern — AI taking jobs — and demonstrate that the centre is built around collaboration with people, upskilling existing workers, and regional economic development. If that case cannot be made, Kill Point 2 stops the project.

05 — Ethics & Sustainability

The constraints we accept

A facility processing research data at this scale needs its limits written down before it opens, not after something goes wrong.

Algorithmic ethics & data governance

ARIC will process large-scale data and machine learning workflows, so an ethical governance structure is embedded in the facility rather than bolted on. All research data is held in accordance with the BC Freedom of Information and Protection of Privacy Act.

Beyond legal compliance, the facility's usage guidelines require an ethical impact assessment for every applied AI project, covering algorithmic bias, data sovereignty, and human-in-the-loop protection measures.

Social equity & inclusive access

ARIC is an open-access facility for UFV students across every discipline, not a restricted site for advanced research alone. Scheduling slots and collaborative spaces are deliberately held back for interdisciplinary use, so that access does not default to whichever department books first.

Energy, water & waste

  • LEED Gold facility engineering. Low-emission structural materials, smart building HVAC management and high-efficiency thermal insulation.
  • Advanced computational cooling. Direct-to-chip liquid cooling in the server rooms, saving up to 40% of operational cooling energy against conventional air-cooled rooms.
  • E-waste & recycling protocols. All decommissioned hardware goes to a certified e-waste partner, with no heavy metals sent to landfill.
